Curated Reading Path · Neuroscience Shelf

Motor Learning & Skill Acquisition

A ranked reading path through the neuroscience shelf, selected by one question: how does the brain acquire movement skills and build motor control? The list favours books with direct usefulness for tennis and tai chi training. Clinical neurology, neuroanatomy, pathology, psychiatry and reference encyclopedias were deliberately set aside even when short and easy to translate.

Compiled from an inventory of 98 neuroscience PDFs, cross-checked against the books already published on the neuroscience shelf. Where a full Vietnamese reader exists, a VI reader link is included.

Deliberately excluded despite being “easy”

Merritt's Neurology (6,103 pp), Fundamentals of Human Neuropsychology (2,880 pp), Atlas of Neuropathology (scan-only), Encyclopedia of Behavioral Neuroscience (2,333 pp), Lectures in Neuroscience (near-empty text layer), Neurodiversity 3e (scan-only) and the clinical / neuroanatomy titles: high translation cost, low relevance to movement-skill training.

NeuroKinetic Therapy (151 pp, scan-only) is a separate explicit task: it has its own reader at /vi/neuroscience/neurokinetic-therapy-weinstock/, produced by OCR rather than the bulk-translation pipeline.

Tier summary — all 98 inventoried books

Scoring combined text-layer quality (0–80), length (3–22), file size (2–10) and topical fit (0–15), with an 18-point penalty for scan-only PDFs. A = score ≥ 108, B = 84–107, C < 84.

  • A (11) — short, clean-text, high fit: Dynamics of Skill Acquisition, Motor Learning and Control for Practitioners, Neuroplasticity (Costandi), The Neuroscience of Emotion, Somatics, A Million Things to Ask a Neuroscientist, Land to Water Yoga, and others.
  • B (62) — moderate: clean but longer or only partly relevant.
  • C (25) — scan-only, very long, or specialist reference / clinical.
